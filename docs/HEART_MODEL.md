# Heartbeat model (BeatNet)

Generated by `python tools/heart_ml/train.py` on 2026-10-22 10:11. Numbers in `mobile/reports/heart-model.json`.

**Experimental, lab only.** The heartbeat is never shown in an emergency and never decides anything: lay rescuers are taught not to check for a pulse.

## What it is

A small neural network (4,057 numbers; four dilated 1-D convolutions that see 1.2 s at a time) that gives the probability of a heartbeat at every moment of the chest's vibration (seismocardiogram). It runs on the phone in plain TypeScript (`mobile/src/sensors/beatnet.ts`), and a test checks that the app's copy gives the same output as the trained one. Two versions: accelerometer only, and accelerometer + gyroscope.

## Data

Real people: the PhysioNet **CEBS** database (Combined measurement of ECG, Breathing and Seismocardiograms; García-González et al., Computing in Cardiology 2013; Open Data Commons Attribution v1.0): healthy volunteers lying still, a chest accelerometer, and beats annotated from the ECG. Used: 41 recordings, 20 people, 1099 minutes, 76,945 beats. Each recording's annotations were checked against R waves found independently in its own ECG: 100.0% agree within 20 ms on the recordings kept.

**A fault in the published database.** Dropped: b002, b003, b004, b005, b006, b007, b008, b009, b010, b011, b012, b013, b014, b015, b016, b017, b018, b019, b020. Their signal files are byte-for-byte copies of p002-p020 (PhysioNet's own `SHA256SUMS.txt` lists the same SHA-256 for each pair), while their annotations and headers are the real basal recordings'. Their annotated beats match the ECG in the file only 2-8% of the time, and their beat-to-beat intervals do not correlate with it at any shift. The first version of this model trained on them, so it was taught, and scored, against beats that were not there: it found 58% of beats. A 10-second window is also skipped wherever the annotations and our R waves disagree on the number of beats.

Simulated people (`mobile/src/sensors/sim.ts`): no heartbeat at all, or footsteps nearby, so the model learns to stay quiet; and, for the gyroscope model, people whose gyroscope feels the heart's twist. No open dataset has a phone-style gyroscope on the chest (one with 29 people needs an IEEE DataPort account: Kaisti et al., DOI 10.21227/vfcs-k196).

## Real people, never seen in training (5-fold cross-validation by person)

10-second windows. A beat counts if it is within 100 ms of an ECG beat after removing the window's median delay (the heart contracts 30-130 ms after the R wave, differently in each person). Phone noise is 180 µg/√Hz (a typical phone accelerometer) added to the recording. "Interval error" is the mean error of beat-to-beat intervals against the ECG's.

## Limits

- CEBS is 20 healthy young adults, lying still, at rest. Nobody in cardiac arrest, nobody heavy, nobody on a soft bed.
- The CEBS sensor is a lab accelerometer taped to the sternum; a phone resting on the chest couples less well. Phone noise is added, but the coupling is not modelled.
- The gyroscope has only simulator evidence.
- Measure it on yourself before trusting any of this (plan in `research/sensor-breathing.md`).
