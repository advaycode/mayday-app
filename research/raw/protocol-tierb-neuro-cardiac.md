## STROKE

### F.A.S.T. (MedlinePlus, NIH/NLM — fetched)
Source: https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000726.htm
- **FACE**: "Ask the person to smile. Check if one side of the face droops."
- **ARMS**: "Ask the person to raise both arms. See if one arm drifts downward."
- **SPEECH**: "Ask the person to repeat a simple sentence. Check if words are slurred."
- **TIME**: "If a person shows any of these symptoms, time is essential."
- Action: **Call 911 immediately.** "the sooner this treatment is started, the better the chance of a good outcome."
- Time window quoted: treatment must begin **within 4½ hours of symptom onset** for maximum effectiveness.
- Other symptoms listed: facial drooping one side; arm weakness/drift; slurred speech; sudden dizziness or vision problems; numbness/tingling one side; loss of balance or coordination; muscle weakness in face/arm/leg usually one side; problems swallowing or understanding speech; sudden severe headache (if caused by bleeding).
- NOTE: this page does **not** cover what-not-to-do (food/drink/aspirin) or positioning. Need another source.

### BE-FAST vs FAST (search-level, needs primary fetch)
- AHA newsroom + PMC12307487: FAST outperformed BE-FAST on **recall** of face/arm/speech; both equally motivated 911 calls, effect held 30 days. BE-FAST adds **B**alance and **E**yes (vision) for greater sensitivity (posterior circulation strokes) but is harder to remember.

## DIABETIC EMERGENCY / HYPOGLYCEMIA

Source: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/preventing-problems/low-blood-glucose-hypoglycemia (NIH NIDDK — fetched)
- Mild-to-moderate symptoms (quoted): "shaky or jittery," "hungry," "tired," "dizzy, lightheaded, confused, or irritable," heart palpitations, headache, vision/speech difficulties.
- Severe symptoms: "lose consciousness" or "have a seizure".
- **15-15 rule**: consume "15 to 20 grams of glucose or carbohydrates right away" when blood glucose is "below your target or less than 70 mg/dL"; "Wait 15 minutes and check your blood glucose level again"; repeat 15–20 g if still low; repeat until in target range.
- Severe (unconscious / cannot swallow): use **glucagon** (injection or nasal spray). Helper should "call 911 right away after giving you glucagon or if you don't have a glucagon emergency kit."
- NIDDK does not explicitly say "no food/drink by mouth if unconscious" — need corroborating source (ADA / Red Cross / AHA).
